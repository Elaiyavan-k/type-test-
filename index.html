How to use TypeFlow

Touch typing is a skill — once learned, you'll type faster than you can think. Here's how to start.

1

Posture & hand position

Sit up straight. Keep your wrists floating — don't rest them on the desk while typing. Your forearms should be parallel to the ground.

Place your fingers on the home row: left hand on A S D F, right hand on J K L ;. The F and J keys have small bumps — feel for them.

2

Don't look at the keys

Keep your eyes on the screen, not the keyboard. Looking down breaks your rhythm and trains the wrong muscle memory. If you can't find a key, slide your fingers back to F and J to recenter.

3

Rhythm over speed

Accuracy first, speed will follow. Aim for steady, even keystrokes — don't hammer keys or pause to think.

  • Press each key with a quick, light tap — let it bounce back
  • Use the correct finger for every key (see the colored diagram above)
  • Keep your hands anchored on the home row between keystrokes
4

The spacebar

Hit the spacebar with your right thumb. Many beginners accidentally hit it with their index finger or palm — keep your thumbs rested on the spacebar between words.

5

Don't fear mistakes

Press Backspace with your right pinky to fix errors. If you miss a word, finish it then go back — never break your flow to correct a single letter.

A 60-second tour of TypeFlow's interface.

⌨️

The typing area

Words appear in the center. The highlighted word is what you type now. Words behind you are dim; words ahead are barely visible. Just start typing — the test starts on your first keystroke.

📊

Live stats

The right panel shows:

  • WPM — words per minute (5 chars = 1 word)
  • Accuracy — % of correctly typed characters
  • Time — seconds remaining
  • Errors — incorrect keystrokes
⚙️

Choosing difficulty

Pick the level that matches your skill:

  • Easy — short common words (3–4 letters). Good for absolute beginners and warm-ups.
  • Medium — everyday English vocabulary. The default.
  • Hard — long words, pangrams, numbers, punctuation. For advanced typists.
📚

Lessons

Structured practice by keyboard row or skill. Pick a lesson to drill a specific area until it becomes muscle memory.

⌨️

Keyboard shortcuts

  • Tab — Restart the current test
  • Space — Move to the next word
  • Backspace — Delete last character
  • Esc — Close this tutorial
📈

Your dashboard

Click Dashboard to see your progress over time, WPM charts, test history, achievements, leaderboard, and daily streak.

💡

Practice daily, even briefly

10 focused minutes a day beats 2 hours once a week. The streak counter helps you stay consistent — come back tomorrow.

🎯

Accuracy beats raw speed

100 WPM at 70% accuracy is slower in real life than 60 WPM at 98% accuracy — because you'll be stopping to fix errors. Aim for 95%+ before pushing for speed.

🔥

Don't chase the WPM counter

If your WPM feels stuck, slow down for a test. Paradoxically, focusing on clean form often unlocks new speed.

👂

Try the sound on

Typewriter audio creates rhythm — you can hear if you're typing evenly. Settings → Sound → Keyboard profile picks your machine.

📅

Use the daily challenge

Same words for everyone today. Compare your score with friends and watch your improvement on the same passage.

🌱

Be patient with yourself

Going from 30 → 60 WPM takes weeks. 60 → 80 takes months. 80 → 100 takes years. Enjoy the journey — every test makes you better.
